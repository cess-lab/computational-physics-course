# Week 01 AI-Enabled Practical: Instructor Protocol

## Purpose

The practical permits generative-AI code writing while assessing whether every student can explain, verify, and interpret the group's work. It uses ten progressive micro-challenges across RC charging, simple harmonic motion, and radioactive decay; each context supplies its model and units.

## Staff workflow

1. Form groups of two or three and record each member in the hidden rotation ledger.
2. At the start, state that AI may be used for code, debugging, or explanation, but that groups must retain only code they can explain and validate.
3. During the investigation phase, require all groups to complete all ten questions in one saved MATLAB file and lock the submission through Classroom before the draw.
4. After lock, assign every group a question slot. If more than ten groups are present, reuse a slot only with a distinct parameter or defect variant from the question bank.
5. Randomly select one eligible student in each group. Use the ledger rule: do not reselect a student in the same group until every member has been selected.
6. For each defence, allow about two minutes for the selected student to explain the model, trace the relevant code/output, and state the conclusion; use about one minute for the variant follow-up.
7. Record the four equal rubric scores immediately. If the selected student is absent, record the absence and schedule the same student for a later make-up defence.
8. Close with a brief correction of any shared misconception; do not require groups to submit a chat transcript.

## Recommended session rhythm

- Use the first half primarily for group investigation and fresh-session reruns.
- Reserve the second half for ten short defences, targeted follow-ups, and class-level feedback. Ten three-minute defences take about 30 minutes; use the remaining time for the draw, transitions, and correction.

## Fairness and integrity rules

- The question slot, selected student, and any parameter/defect variant are drawn only after the group evidence is locked.
- A group cannot nominate its speaker.
- A selected student may use the group's submitted work but may not obtain a new AI response during the spoken defence.
- A defence tests explanation and verification, not memorisation of MATLAB function names.
- Treat the individual score as evidence of current understanding, not as an AI-detection exercise.

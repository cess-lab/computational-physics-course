# Individual Defence and Rotation Protocol

This protocol operationalises the blueprint rules for the practical portfolio, individual lab test, and capstone. It is an instructor record/procedure, not a student answer key.

## Common evidence expected in a defence

The selected student should be able to:

1. state the physical question, assumptions, variables, and units;
2. explain the relevant pseudocode or numerical update;
3. trace one supplied or modified code section and connect it to the output;
4. justify the required validation check and interpret its outcome; and
5. answer one short parameter-change, defect, physics, or limitation follow-up.

AI-assisted work also requires a concise decision record: tool used, requested assistance, accepted/modified/rejected material, and independent check. A complete chat history is not required, and AI-detection scores are not evidence of misconduct.

## Practical portfolio and capstone rotation

1. Students work in groups of two or three. Before submission is locked, groups complete the required evidence packet; no defence slot is drawn early enough to change the submitted evidence.
2. After the packet is locked, draw one question slot and one eligible group member. If a cohort has more groups than slots, reuse a slot only with a distinct post-submission parameter or defect variant.
3. Maintain a hidden ledger with at least: group ID, member, assessment, question slot, parameter/defect variant, date, status, evidence location, and next-eligible cycle.
4. Within a group, do not select a member again until every member has one completed defence entry for the current cycle. This means every member has one completed entry before any member receives a second entry.
5. An absence is **pending**, not a zero or a completed entry. Arrange a later make-up defence and keep the student eligible only after the make-up is completed.
6. For the practical portfolio, the completed individual entries form the 3% cumulative defence ledger. Average each student’s completed entries after the first-completion condition has been satisfied; retain pending absences until their make-up.
7. For the capstone, record the individual defence and the clean reproducibility handoff in the 2% capstone component. Each member must explain a relevant code section even if another member performed the final run.

## Individual lab-test handling

Each student completes an individual defence attached to the 10% lab test. The lecturer may issue a fresh parameter or clear defect variant during the defence. Record the student’s variant and completion status. If absent, mark the defence pending and arrange a make-up; do not treat the missing defence as evidence of failure of the code packet alone.

## Marker safeguards

- Keep student-facing instructions separate from answer keys and confidential final-examination material.
- Assess explanation, tracing, validation, and physical reasoning; do not assess accent, speed, or polished code style as proxies for understanding.
- Record a precise reason when a defence is deferred or a packet is returned for revision.
- Do not upload this protocol, answer keys, or final-examination questions to the student NotebookLM source set.

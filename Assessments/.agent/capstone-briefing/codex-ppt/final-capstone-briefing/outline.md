## Slide outline

| # | Title | Key points and visual role |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Present, Defend and Hand Off Your Investigation | Introduce the Week 13–14 end point: final simulation, evidence-led presentation, individual defence and reproducible handoff. Cover. |
| 2 | What the Final Assessment Rewards | Physical relevance, defensible model reasoning, numerical implementation, evidence/validation, critical analysis, reproducibility and individual understanding; complexity alone is not rewarded. Assessment orientation. |
| 3 | Turn the Scenario into a Defensible Claim | State the real-world objective, selected model, approximation and claim boundary. Narrative framing. |
| 4 | Show the Model You Chose and Why | Included/omitted physics, assumptions, parameter sources, initial/boundary conditions and why scope is appropriate. Model-defence layout. |
| 5 | Explain the Realism Trade-Off | Compare lecture-level baseline and realistic extension; explain physical improvement, data/cost trade-offs and remaining omissions. Layered-model comparison. |
| 6 | Make the Numerical Implementation Traceable | Explain algorithm, discretisation/solver, physical terms in code, numerical settings and implementation decisions. Code-to-physics layout. |
| 7 | Choose Evidence That Answers the Objective | Students choose appropriate graphs, tables, animations or other evidence; label units, cases, scales and assumptions; distinguish model output from measured/supplied data. Evidence design. |
| 8 | Validate the Physics and the Computation | Match tests to the model and claim: units, limiting/reference behaviour, convergence/sensitivity, physical plausibility, data comparison and reproducibility. No universal pair of checks. Validation matrix. |
| 9 | Show Sensitivity and Uncertainty Honestly | Identify parameters/settings that affect conclusions; distinguish parameter uncertainty, numerical sensitivity, data limitations and model-form limitations. Robustness layout. |
| 10 | Explain What the Simulation Cannot Establish | Link omissions, unreliable regimes, data gaps and resolution limits to the strength of the conclusion. Critical-analysis slide. |
| 11 | Record AI Decisions Transparently | ChatGPT, MATLAB Copilot or other declared tools; accepted/modified/rejected assistance; reasons and independent checks; no full chat history. Responsible-AI record. |
| 12 | Prepare a Reproducible Handoff | Code, data, parameter sources, units, environment/release, run order, numerical settings, output instructions, tests and known limitations. Handoff bundle. |
| 13 | Use an Evidence-Led Presentation Structure | Objective and scenario → model and realism decision → algorithm/implementation → evidence/validation → interpretation, limitation and conclusion. Presentation flow. |
| 14 | Prepare for the Individual Defence | Each member explains a model/code choice, traces numerical reasoning, defends a test, and answers a parameter, limitation or realism follow-up. Defence rehearsal. |
| 15 | Final Lock and Submission Checklist | Model/evidence documented; tests and sensitivity recorded; AI record present; clean rerun succeeds; handoff usable; every member ready; submit through Google Classroom. Closing checklist. |

## Timing note

Deck B is taught in Week 07 or 08. Weeks 09–12 use it as a working target. A short reminder/checklist may be reused immediately before Week 13 presentations, but Week 13 introduces no new briefing content.

## Content boundaries

Do not prescribe one graph, one numerical method, one parameter change, one algorithm template, one pair of validation checks, or one common code structure. The deck teaches students how to defend their own choices against the physical objective.

No strict raster assets are required. If verified code, equations, data, or figures are used in a sample, preserve them as strict source inputs.

## Density rules

- Slides 01–05 and 12–14 are concise-to-moderate briefing slides with one clear visual message.
- Slides 06–11 are intentionally information-dense because they are the twelve project reference briefs.
- Slides 06–11 use a consistent dense structure: **Context + objective → Possible realism dimensions → Likely information → Boundary questions**.
- Briefs 09–10 are capstone-level Monte Carlo investigations. The course blueprint places Monte Carlo sampling in Week 10; statistical-mechanics simulation is a Stretch direction, so the brief should provide a bounded scaffold and assess physical interpretation, evidence and reproducibility rather than derivation of the algorithm.
- Keep the project detail readable through strong grouping, short lines, clear hierarchy and the approved illustrated scientific direction; do not remove the information merely to make the slide empty.
- No week-by-week teaching explanation.
- No student-facing wording that implies groups choose a project or model.
- Possible realism dimensions are examples, not a compulsory checklist. Each brief should communicate a defensible Core scope and boundary questions.

## Visual direction

Use a semi-realistic illustrated scientific briefing style: clean editorial forms, clear outlines, soft shading, gentle depth, restrained perspective and material cues, but no photographic people, stock-photo appearance or glossy photorealism. Keep the approved navy/white/blue/teal/ochre/coral palette, readable sans-serif hierarchy, clean icons, and purposeful diagrams. Use richer illustrated scene graphics for the six brief slides and simpler but still illustrated process graphics for the other slides.

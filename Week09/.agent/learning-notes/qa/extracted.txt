Data Fitting and Uncertainty:
From Measurements to Parameters
Use a small physical dataset to estimate a parameter, inspect where the model misses the data, and report
only the uncertainty the evidence supports.
Physical question. A resistor is labelled 47 Ω, but measured voltage and current are not perfectly
exact. What resistance is supported by the full dataset, how can residuals reveal model mismatch,
and how precise should the reported answer be?

Core learning outcomes
1. fit a supplied simple physical relationship and interpret each fitted parameter with its unit;
2. calculate and interpret residuals to judge whether a model misses visible structure; and
3. validate a fitted result and state uncertainty cautiously using a supplied repeatability range.

1. Measurements are not the same as model parameters
The experiment supplies pairs of current I and voltage V . These are measured quantities. We propose
the simple relationship
V = RI + b,
where R is the resistance and b is a small voltage offset. The values of R and b are fitted parameters:
they are inferred from the pattern across all measurements.

3. A fitting algorithm in plain language
For this week, the least-squares calculation itself is supplied. The Core reasoning is the sequence around
it:
1. plot the measured data with labelled axes and units;
2. state the proposed relationship and identify the parameter units;
3. fit a straight line to all measured pairs;
4. evaluate the fitted model at the measured current values;
5. calculate residual = measured voltage − fitted voltage;
6. inspect whether the residuals scatter around zero or show a pattern;
7. validate the fitted resistance against independent physical evidence; and
8. report only the precision supported by the repeatability evidence.

The absence of obvious structure supports the straight-line model over this measured range. It does not
prove that the resistor is perfectly ohmic at every current or temperature.
Core model-quality check. Small residuals are not enough by themselves. Ask whether they
show a pattern. A U-shaped sequence—positive at both ends and negative in the middle—would
suggest missing curvature or another systematic effect.

7. Validate the fitted resistance with a physical reference
The resistor is labelled 47 Ω with a nominal 5% tolerance. The manufacturer range is therefore
47(1 − 0.05) = 44.65 Ω,

47(1 + 0.05) = 49.35 Ω.

The fitted value 47.0000 Ω lies inside this range. This is an independent physical/reference check because
the nominal component information did not come from the fitting calculation.
Tolerance is not fit uncertainty. The 5% label describes the component specification. It
does not tell us how much the fitted resistance would change if we repeated this measurement
procedure. Keep these two ideas separate.

This sequence is more important than memorising a fitting function name. In an assessment, the syntax
may be supplied; you still need to explain the physical parameter, unit, residual evidence, and justified
conclusion.

Working exposure: one fit-quality statistic
You may be shown a supplied coeﬀicient of determination, R2 . Values closer to 1 indicate that the fitted
predictions account for more of the observed variation in that dataset. Treat it as supporting evidence
only. A high R2 does not repair wrong units, a poor physical model, a structured residual pattern, or
an unverified data-entry error.
You may also be shown two plausible simple models and their residual evidence. Compare the supplied
evidence; independent reproduction of advanced selection metrics is not required.

Optional stretch
Weighted fitting, nonlinear fitting, covariance matrices, regularisation, and formal confidence intervals
are outside the Week 9 Core route. They are not required for ordinary pass-level work here.

Three takeaways
1. A fitted parameter must keep the unit implied by the physical equation.
2. Residuals show where the model misses the measurements; visible structure can reveal a poor model
even when the overlay looks convincing.
3. Report uncertainty only to the level supported by the evidence, and keep repeatability distinct from
manufacturer tolerance or formal confidence intervals.
Preparation for the practical. Be ready to fit a supplied straight-line scaffold, state parameter
units, read residual structure, use one independent check, and report uncertainty only to justified
precision.

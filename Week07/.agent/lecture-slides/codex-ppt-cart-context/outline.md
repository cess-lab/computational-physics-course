- `\mathrm{N}\times\mathrm{s}=\mathrm{N\,s}=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}`
- `F(t)=F_0 e^{-t/\tau}`

## Slide sequence

### Slide 01 - From a Curve to an Accumulated Quantity

- Introduce the physical question: how can sampled force values estimate total impulse from `t=0` to `2.0 s`?
- Establish the phrase `area under force-time curve = impulse`.
- Visual: strong title composition with a clean decaying-force curve and accumulated area, grounded in `week07_impulse_trapezoids.png`.
- Strict equation: `J=\int_0^T F(t)\,dt`.

### Slide 02 - A Cart Driven by a Fading Thrust

- Concrete setup: a compressed-air reservoir drives a `2.0 kg` cart along a low-friction track.
- As the reservoir empties, pressure and thrust decrease.
- A force sensor records `F(t)` over the `2.0 s` push.
- The area under the measured force-time curve is the cart's impulse.
- Visual: side-view cart, reservoir/nozzle, thrust arrow, force sensor, and a small decaying-force trace.

### Slide 03 - Integration Combines Two Physical Axes

- Force axis carries `N`; time axis carries `s`.
- Area carries `N s`, equivalent to `kg m/s`.
- Limits `0 <= t <= 2.0 s` define what interval is accumulated.
- Prompt: if force stays positive, what sign must the impulse have?
- Visual: axis-unit multiplication map with the integration limits clearly attached.

### Slide 04 - One Decaying-Force Model

- Show `F(t)=12 exp(-t/0.8) N` over `0 <= t <= 2.0 s`.
- Force remains positive and decreases continuously.
- Prediction: `0 < J < 12 x 2 = 24 N s`.
- Include the exact parameter code from the lecture demonstration.
- Visual: smooth force curve compared with the `24 N s` bounding rectangle.

### Slide 05 - From Reservoir Pressure to Momentum

- Reservoir empties -> pressure falls -> thrust decreases.
- Force sensor samples the changing thrust.
- Each trapezoid adds a small momentum increment `Delta J`.
- The accumulated impulse changes the cart's momentum: `J = Delta p`.
- Visual: one causal process chain from reservoir to momentum.

### Slide 06 - A Numerical Integral Sees Samples

- Four equal intervals give five coordinates: `0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 s`.
- Exact force samples: `12.0000, 6.4231, 3.4381, 1.8403, 0.9850 N`.
- Include the exact `linspace` and force-evaluation code from the lecture demonstration.
- Visual: five markers and vertical guides mapped clearly to the five-value table.

### Slide 07 - One Trapezoid

- Replace the curve locally by a straight segment between adjacent samples.
- Area equals width multiplied by average endpoint height.
- Use `Delta t = 0.5 s`, `F_0 = 12.0000 N`, and `F_1 = 6.4231 N`.
- Worked value: `A_1 approximately 4.6058 N s`.
- Visual: enlarged first trapezoid with width and endpoint heights labelled unambiguously.

### Slide 08 - Four Trapezoids Form the Coarse Estimate

- Show endpoint weights `1/2, 1, 1, 1, 1/2` aligned with the five samples.
- Composite result: `9.0969821492 N s`.
- Include the exact manual MATLAB expression from the lecture demonstration.
- Check: `0 < 9.09698 < 24 N s` passes the physical bound but does not yet establish accuracy.
- Visual: full four-trapezoid accumulation. This is the preferred representative sample slide.

### Slide 09 - Pseudocode Before MATLAB

- State limits and expected unit.
- Choose a resolution and create sample coordinates.
- Evaluate the model, form trapezoid areas, and add them.
- Refine, compare with a reference, then interpret.
- Visual: one clear process with a loop from comparison back to resolution.
- Retrieval prompt: which quantities stay fixed during refinement?

### Slide 13 - Error With Physical Meaning

- Finest estimate: `8.8299047499 N s`.

### Slide 14 - Impulse Predicts the Cart's Final Speed

- Assume a `2.0 kg` cart starts from rest on a low-friction track.
- `J = Delta p = m Delta v`.
- `Delta v = 8.8119840132 / 2.0 = 4.4059920066 m/s`.
- Predicted final speed: approximately `4.41 m/s`.
- State the constant-mass and negligible-resistance assumptions.

- Students read the comparison only. Do not derive Simpson's rule or state a formal error-order theorem.
- Visual: method comparison plus the retained refinement evidence.
